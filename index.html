About the course

Why this course

Of the 281 million international migrants worldwide, 169 million are migrant workers. They contribute fully to the economies of their host and home countries, yet they are often excluded from social protection coverage.

Compared with nationals who work their entire lives in one country, migrant workers face major legal and practical obstacles in accessing social protection benefits. They may be denied access, or have limited access, to social security because of their status or nationality, or because of the insufficient duration of their periods of employment and residence. Maintaining earned benefits and benefits in the course of acquisition is particularly important for long-term benefits (invalidity, old-age and survivors’), where qualifying periods may be considerable.

This course focuses on the different unilateral, bilateral and regional measures that exist to extend social protection to both migrant workers and refugees and their families, in their countries of destination and origin.

Objectives

What will I be able to do?

  1. 1

    Understand the issues and opportunities related to the extension of social protection to migrant workers, refugees and their families.

  2. 2

    Appreciate the international legal framework covering migrant workers and refugees.

  3. 3

    Analyze the different measures existing to extend social protection to migrant workers and refugees, including multilateral and bilateral agreements and unilateral measures in both countries of origin and destination.

  4. 4

    Examine the specific challenges faced by certain groups in order to access social protection, such as domestic workers, temporary migrant workers, irregular migrants and refugees.

  5. 5

    Explore and analyze how to extend social protection to migrants and refugees based on country experiences, good practices and lessons learned from various other countries.

Learning approach

How the course works

The course consists of 8 online modules offered through the ITCILO e-Campus online platform, to be completed over six weeks for an estimated 60 learning hours. It is broken down into three phases.

  1. Phase 1 · Asynchronous

    Flexible learning

    Self-guided online learning on e-Campus, forum of discussion facilitated by experts, and assessment throughout the different phases of the course.

  2. Phase 2 · Synchronous

    “Real time” learning

    Live interactive sessions and engaging video presentations by highly experienced trainers, blended with individual and collaborative group exercises, peer-to-peer assessment and online technical forums on e-Campus.

  3. Phase 3 · Asynchronous

    End of course assignment

    An individual assignment applying the teachings acquired during the course.

  • Global perspective

    An action-oriented, highly participative approach, with particular attention to sharing international experiences with a view to their adaptation and practical application, including “good practices” presented through case studies.

  • Integrated e-learning

    An innovative e-learning experience combining self-paced modules, webinars and discussions, case studies, forum debates, role-play exercises and group work.

  • Peer-to-peer learning

    Practitioners from different parts of the world and different backgrounds join the e-learning to share their concrete experience with participants.

    Who attends this course

    The course is designed for officials and practitioners committed to extending social protection to migrant workers and refugees through multilateral, bilateral and unilateral measures. More precisely:

    • 1

      Managers, planners, advisers and professionals working in social security institutions

    • 2

      Policy-planners and officials from key ministries responsible for migration and/or social protection

    • 3

      Representatives of the social partners involved in the governance of migration and/or social security institutions

    • 4

      Practitioners and consultants of UN agencies
